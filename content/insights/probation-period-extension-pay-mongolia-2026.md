Under Mongolian law, the **initial probationary employment period cannot exceed three months**. It may be **extended once, for no more than another three months**, by mutual agreement. A probationary employee remains entitled to wages and the protection of labour legislation. Probation does not give an employer an unrestricted right to reduce pay or dismiss an employee without a lawful basis. These rules arise directly from [Article 64 of Mongolia's Labour Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751).

## What is the purpose of a probationary employment contract?

Article 64.1 permits an employer to conclude a probationary employment contract to assess whether a newly hired person meets the requirements of the position. The purpose is to evaluate actual competence, work performance and suitability for the role. It is not a general exemption from the obligations ordinarily imposed on employers.

A probationary contract must be distinguished from an apprenticeship arrangement, temporary fixed-term employment and a civil-law services contract. Where a person performs the duties of a job under the employer's management in return for wages, the actual employment relationship must be assessed under labour legislation. Merely labelling the agreement “probation” does not determine its legal character.

## Can an employer prescribe six months from the outset?

Under Article 64.2, the **first period is limited to three months**. The parties may subsequently **agree to a single extension not exceeding three months**. A clause simply imposing an unconditional six-month probation period from the beginning therefore carries a risk of conflicting with the two-stage arrangement contemplated by the statute.

For example, suppose an employee starts work on 1 November 2026 under an initial three-month probationary contract. Before that period ends, the parties should assess the results and discuss whether an extension is justified. A unilateral order declaring that probation “has been extended for three months” does not, by itself, establish mutual agreement. Recording the extension in writing or another legally valid electronic form creates a clearer evidential record.

## Can probationary staff be paid less than other employees?

Article 64.3 requires the **basic salary of a probationary employee to be no lower than the basic salary of the relevant position**. Additional wages, premiums, incentives and allowances must also be paid in accordance with the law. If the basic salary for a particular position is MNT 2,500,000, paying a probationer a basic salary of MNT 1,500,000 solely because of probation calls for scrutiny under Article 64.3.

Under Article 64.5, labour legislation, collective agreements, collective bargaining arrangements and internal employment norms **apply equally** to probationary employees. Employers should therefore observe the rules on recording hours, payment dates, rest periods, workplace safety and statutory additional remuneration. The argument that a person is “not yet a permanent employee” is not a legal reason to disregard those protections.

## When is a probationary contract prohibited?

Article 64.4 rules out probationary contracts where a person is hired to perform a one-off seasonal job, replace an employee whose position is being preserved, or occupy a temporary position. The appropriate form of employment in those circumstances must be determined under the applicable provisions governing temporary or fixed-term work.

For instance, a person engaged to cover another employee's parental leave until that employee returns should not be placed on a probationary contract merely for convenience. The employer must correctly identify the position, the purpose of the appointment and the period for which the substitute is employed.

## Can an employer immediately dismiss someone who “failed probation”?

Probation is **not a substitute for every statutory ground of termination**. Articles 78 and 80 of the Labour Law regulate the end of employment and termination at the employer's initiative. Article 80.1.2 specifically addresses an employee's demonstrated lack of requisite professional qualifications, skills or performance and provides that the employee should have been warned in advance and given a reasonable opportunity to improve.

A decision based on a vague statement that an employee “did not satisfy management” is therefore much weaker than one supported by the job description, assigned work, objective performance criteria, assessment records and evidence of feedback, warnings and an opportunity to improve. The legal consequences of expiry of a fixed probationary period and an employer's decision to terminate before expiry must be examined separately. The precise contractual arrangement and grounds relied upon matter.

## What deadlines apply if an employee challenges the decision?

Employees should retain their contract, any extension, payslips, work assignments, performance assessments, termination notice and proof of the date when the notice was received. Under **Article 154.2.1**, a challenge to an employer's decision terminating or ending employment must generally be brought before the relevant employment-dispute mechanism **within 30 days of receipt**. Other employment-rights disputes are subject to the **90-day** period under Article 154.2.2. The proper sequence of proceedings, including any preliminary dispute-resolution body and recourse to the courts, must be read together with Articles 154–158.

The practical questions are therefore not limited to the number of months worked. One must identify the contract originally signed, whether the extension was genuinely agreed, whether the salary complied with the rate for the position, what legal ground the employer cited and when the employee received the decision.
