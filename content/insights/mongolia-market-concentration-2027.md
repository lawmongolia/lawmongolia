Mergers and share acquisitions in Mongolia should not be analysed solely under company law. Where a transaction involves a business with a significant position in a relevant market, competition-law review may also become relevant.

An amendment dated 2 July 2026 introduced a new article 5.4 to the Competition Law, requiring the Government to approve a methodology and related rules for determining market concentration. The provision becomes effective on 1 January 2027.

## What market concentration means

The Competition Law defines market concentration by reference to the share of a product market held by an undertaking alone, together with others, or together with its related parties. It treats market power separately, as the ability of an undertaking to influence the market.

Under the current rules, an undertaking is treated as dominant where it accounts for one-third or more of production, sales or purchases in a particular product market. Dominance may also be established below that threshold where an undertaking has the ability to prevent market entry or exclude competitors, taking into account the relevant product market, geographic market, concentration and market power.

The new article 5.4 lays the basis for a more structured assessment. The methodology and rules to be approved by the Government from 2027 are likely to matter considerably in practice.

## This is not a universal merger-filing requirement

There is an important distinction here. The addition of a market-concentration methodology from 2027 does not create a general system under which every share acquisition in Mongolia is notified to the Antimonopoly Agency.

Article 8.1 of the Competition Law requires a dominant legal entity to apply to the Antimonopoly Agency where it merges or consolidates with another entity, where it acquires more than 20 per cent of the common shares or more than 15 per cent of the preferred shares of a competing company selling the same type of product, and where it merges or consolidates with a related party.

The Agency has 30 days to review the application and may extend that period by up to a further 30 days. Where it concludes that the transaction would create conditions restricting competition, it may issue an adverse opinion, which can prevent registration of the corporate reorganisation.

The Law on State Registration of Legal Entities likewise includes the Antimonopoly Agency's opinion under article 8.1 among the documents required on a reorganisation by merger or consolidation.

## What to check before a transaction

In practice, the early-stage question is not the target's revenue or assets. It is which product and geographic markets the target operates in, and what position it holds in them.

Where the purchaser and the target operate in the same product or service market, market shares and the sales of related parties have to be aggregated. From 2027, the market-concentration methodology should give that assessment clearer quantitative criteria.

## Conclusion

Competition review is not a separate issue confined to monopolies. Where a party to an acquisition, merger or consolidation holds a significant market position, it can bear directly on the transaction timetable, the closing conditions and the state registration process.

The market-concentration rules taking effect on 1 January 2027 provide the legal basis for a more systematic assessment. On substantial transactions, the competition check therefore belongs at the structuring stage rather than at the end of due diligence.
