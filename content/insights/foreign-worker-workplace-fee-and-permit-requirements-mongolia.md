Employing a foreign national in Mongolia is subject to several distinct statutory requirements under the Law on Labour Force Migration. In addition to employment, visa and residence formalities, an employer may need to comply with the workplace fee regime, annual limits on foreign workers, invitation requirements and employment-permit rules.

These requirements operate independently and should therefore be assessed separately.

## Workplace fee and statutory exemptions

Article 32.1 of the Law on Labour Force Migration provides that, unless otherwise stipulated by law, the monthly workplace fee for a foreign employee is equal to twice the applicable monthly minimum wage.

For 2026, the officially published amount is **MNT 1,584,000 per employee per month**.

Article 32.3 nevertheless provides exemptions for specified employees and employers. These include certain foreign professionals engaged by educational, scientific, healthcare, cultural and sports organisations, as well as employers engaging foreign workers in crop farming, advanced technology, food production, light industry, tourism and service sectors.

The financial impact can be significant. For example, the workplace fee alone for ten non-exempt foreign employees would amount to MNT 15.84 million per month.

Eligibility should not, however, be determined merely by how a company describes its business. Its actual economic activity, the employee’s position and relevant registration and regulatory documentation should be considered together.

## A workplace fee exemption does not remove permit requirements

A workplace fee exemption should not be confused with an exemption from the foreign-employment permit regime.

Under Mongolian law, permission to invite a foreign worker, an employment permit, the workplace fee and annual numerical and proportional limits on foreign workers are separate legal requirements.

Relief from the workplace fee therefore does not, by itself, authorise employment without the required permits.

Under the ordinary procedure, the employer must first register the vacancy and relevant job requirements and seek to recruit a Mongolian national.

Unless a statutory exception applies, where a suitable domestic employee cannot be recruited within **14 working days** after active recruitment, the employer may apply for permission to invite a foreign worker.

Following invitation approval, the employer enters into an employment contract with the selected foreign national and completes the employment-permit procedure.

## An employment permit is tied to the authorised employer, work and location

A foreign employment permit is not a general right to work anywhere in Mongolia.

The permit is connected to a specified employer, employee, work, location and period. Depending on the employment contract and position, it may be issued for a period of up to one year.

Article 26.1.6 provides grounds for cancellation where a foreign employee is assigned to work for a purpose or at a location different from that authorised by the permit, or where false documents were used in obtaining the permit.

Accordingly, transferring a foreign employee between affiliated companies, projects, work locations or materially different functions should not be treated merely as an internal human-resources decision. The regulatory implications should first be assessed.

## Annual limits apply independently

Article 22.1 requires the Government to determine annually the number and proportion of foreign workers permitted by sector of economic activity.

For 2026, the applicable limits were approved by Government Resolution No. 113 dated 1 October 2025.

An employer should therefore verify separately:

1. whether it qualifies for a workplace fee exemption;
2. whether a sectoral numerical or proportional limit applies;
3. whether permission to invite the foreign worker is required;
4. whether the employee has obtained the necessary employment permit; and
5. whether the actual employment remains within the employer, position, location and period authorised by that permit.

Compliance with one requirement does not remove the others.

## Permit violations may affect future recruitment

Certain violations may result in the employer’s right to invite foreign workers being restricted for **up to two years**.

For businesses that depend materially on foreign engineers, technical specialists or other highly skilled personnel, such a restriction may create greater commercial consequences than a one-off administrative fine.

Foreign-worker compliance should therefore form part of the company’s broader regulatory and business-continuity framework.

## Orientation for first-time foreign workers

Article 31.1 requires an employer to arrange orientation for a foreign employee coming to Mongolia for work for the first time.

The orientation must cover relevant Mongolian legislation, customs and other information necessary for employment, and the related cost is borne by the employer.

Accordingly, foreign-worker onboarding should not end with execution of the employment contract and completion of visa and residence procedures. Employment permits, orientation requirements and continuing compliance with permit conditions should be managed through an integrated internal process.

## Conclusion

The central feature of Mongolia’s foreign-worker regime is that several independent requirements operate simultaneously.

An exemption from the workplace fee does not constitute an exemption from invitation or employment-permit requirements. Likewise, the existence of an employment permit does not authorise the employer to disregard annual sectoral limits or the employer, work and location specified in that permit.

Businesses intending to employ foreign nationals should therefore review **the workplace fee and available exemptions, annual sectoral limits, invitation approval, employment permit requirements and continuing compliance with permit conditions** as separate legal matters.
