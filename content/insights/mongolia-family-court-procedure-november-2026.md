Mongolia adopted a dedicated [Law on Adjudicating Family Cases](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=17438180618972) on 3 July 2026. It provides a more specialised procedural framework for divorce, custody, parental contact, maintenance and family-property disputes, with particular attention to children's rights and safety. **The statute is not yet generally operative on 19 October 2026.** Article 42.1 sets **1 November 2026** as the principal commencement date, while specified provisions are deferred until 1 January 2027.

The distinction between adoption, commencement and the transitional rules matters to anyone seeking a family-law advocate in Mongolia in late 2026.

## What cases does the specialised law cover?

Article 5.1 identifies disputes concerning marriage, parents and children, custody and contact, maintenance, adoption, guardianship and family assets. A divorce case is no longer viewed simply as deciding whether a marriage should end; it may require a structured decision about the child's living arrangements, contact with a parent and financial support.

However, Article 1 of the [Commencement and Transitional Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=17438220176752) delays Article 5.1.11 (property relations arising from unregistered cohabitation), Articles 21–25 and Chapter Four until **1 January 2027**. It would be inaccurate to say that every provision takes effect in November.

## Custody, contact and the child's views

Articles 17.1–17.4 require a divorce claim to address the relevant requests concerning custody, contact, maintenance and property, together with any arrangements already agreed between the parties. The court is to decide custody, contact and maintenance alongside the divorce claim. For children aged **seven or older**, the child's views and a report by a child-and-family specialist are relevant considerations.

This does not give a seven-year-old the final legal choice of residence. The amended [Family Law, new Article 14¹](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=17438169143722), directs attention to the child's best interests, including development, relationships with family members, the parents' actual caregiving, living arrangements and the impact of domestic violence.

## Interim protection while the case is pending

Article 19 authorises specified **interim measures** where necessary. Depending on the circumstances, these may concern temporary residence of a child, interim contact arrangements, support payments, restrictions on contact in situations involving safety risks or restraints on disposing of a family home or jointly held property.

These measures are not final determinations of custody or ownership. Their purpose is to protect rights and safety before the court resolves the merits. Certain measures may be ordered without prior notice to the affected party, subject to the statutory process for receiving explanations afterwards. An applicant should distinguish immediate risks from longer-term substantive claims and present the supporting evidence accordingly.

## Is mediation compulsory in every case?

Article 16.1 introduces a pre-court process for divorce disputes, subject to the exceptions referred to in Article 26.4. It is important to distinguish that mechanism from the new judicial mediation framework in **Chapter Four**, whose commencement is deferred until January 2027. Cases involving violence or a serious safety concern require particular care; general statements about obligatory mediation should not override the statutory exceptions or safeguards.

The right approach is to determine which provisions apply to the particular case on the date the process is initiated, rather than treating the new Act as a single undifferentiated set of rules.

## What happens to proceedings already filed?

Article 2 of the [Transitional Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=17438220176752) provides that family cases commenced, and claims received, before the new statute comes into force are to be handled under the previous procedural framework by the relevant courts. A hearing date after 1 November is not by itself sufficient to make all of the new provisions applicable. The filing and acceptance dates should therefore be verified.

## Preparing for legal advice

A person instructing a family-law advocate should organise marriage and birth records, evidence of caregiving, relevant income and child expenses, property ownership and financing documents, and reliable records of any immediate safety issues. Particular care is needed to protect a child's privacy and avoid gathering evidence through unlawful access to another person's accounts or devices.

Divorce, custody, maintenance and property claims do not all turn on the same facts. Sound legal representation begins by separating those issues and identifying the applicable transitional provisions.
