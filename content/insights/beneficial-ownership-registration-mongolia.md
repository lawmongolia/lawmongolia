The shareholder shown in Mongolia's corporate registry does not necessarily identify the person who ultimately owns or controls the company. This becomes particularly important where ownership is held through several holding companies, foreign entities or nominee-type arrangements.

Under Mongolia's Law on Combating Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing, a beneficial owner is not confined to the direct registered shareholder. The concept extends to the natural person who ultimately owns a significant interest, directs or controls the legal entity, or benefits from the entity by exercising control over its transactions and operations.

## The ownership chain must be traced to a natural person

Mongolia's legal-entity registration rules require an ownership chain to be traced through intermediate legal entities until the ultimate natural person is identified.

So where a Mongolian company's shares are held by foreign company A, A is held by company B, and B is in turn held by one or more individuals, naming company A does not complete the registration. The chain has to be followed through to the individuals.

Where foreign entities form part of that chain, supporting corporate documents certified by the competent authority of the relevant country, together with certified translations, may be required. A foreign individual identified as the beneficial owner may also need to provide a passport copy.

## Changes must be registered within 15 business days

Beneficial ownership is not registered once and forgotten. A share sale, a change of investor or a restructuring at parent level may all change the beneficial owner.

Where the beneficial owner of a legal entity changes, the relevant decision and supporting documentation must generally be submitted to the registration authority within 15 business days.

Signing a share transfer agreement and updating the direct shareholder in the registry is therefore not necessarily the end of the exercise. Whether the transaction changes the beneficial owner needs to be checked separately.

## Where the gap usually arises

The common compliance gap is treating a change at parent level as irrelevant in Mongolia because the direct shareholder has not changed. Because the registration framework requires the chain to be traced to a natural person, an upstream transaction may still require an update in Mongolia.

Equally, a person who holds no shares in their own name but who in practice directs the company's decisions, controls its transactions or receives its benefits should not be overlooked. The law defines beneficial ownership by reference to control and benefit, not only formal shareholding.

## The simplest check a company can run

On every share sale, new investment or group restructuring, three questions belong together. Has the direct shareholder changed? Has anything changed at any level of the ownership chain? And as a result, has the natural person who actually controls the company or receives its benefits changed?

Beneficial ownership is not only a formal registration requirement. It is among the first things examined in bank customer due diligence, investment due diligence, financing, licensing and anti-money-laundering supervision.

## Conclusion

Maintaining beneficial ownership information properly means more than repeating the name on the share certificate. It means establishing, transparently and with supporting evidence, who ultimately controls the entity and who receives its benefits.

Checking whether a change in shareholding or investment structure affects the beneficial ownership record belongs on the closing checklist. It is the simplest and most effective protection available.
