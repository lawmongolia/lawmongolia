When a parent company wants an employee to work for a subsidiary, or one subsidiary plans to transfer staff to another, the first question is often whether **an appointment letter and an amendment to the existing employment contract** will be enough. Companies in a group may share owners, executives, an office or internal HR processes, but they remain separate legal persons. The identity of the employer and the allocation of employment obligations therefore matter.

Under [Article 47.1 of Mongolia's Labour Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751), an employer and employee enter into an employment contract by agreement. Article 52.1 permits the parties to amend that contract by mutual agreement. Neither provision means that a bilateral amendment between an employee and Company A automatically makes a separate Company B the employee's new employer. The parties should first determine **what kind of arrangement they actually intend**.

## 1. Is the legal employer changing?

Intercompany movements may fall into several different models. An employee may leave Company A and enter a new employment contract with Company B. Alternatively, the employee may remain with Company A and contribute to a particular project or service provided to Company B. A more structured arrangement could fall within the statutory rules governing labour-supply services, where the legal conditions are satisfied.

The models are not interchangeable. The correct analysis asks who exercises day-to-day supervision, who pays wages, who maintains the employment records, whose workplace rules apply, who assesses performance, who can take disciplinary action and how long the arrangement will last.

A label such as “secondment”, “assignment” or “management services” cannot substitute for examining the real rights and obligations.

## 2. Permanent move to a new employer: termination and rehire

Where the intended result is for the employee to become a regular employee of Company B, a clear solution is often to **end the employment relationship with Company A on a lawful basis** and enter a new employment contract with Company B.

[Article 78.1.1 of the Labour Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751) recognises termination of employment by mutual agreement. In that case, the employee's genuine agreement, the termination date, outstanding wages, accrued leave, return of company property and handover of information should be recorded. Company B's contract should set out its own legal name, position, duties, remuneration, working conditions and commencement date.

[Article 48.1](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751) requires employment contracts to be made in writing or electronically and signed by the parties. A properly executed new contract reduces the risk of later uncertainty about which company is responsible for salary, working conditions and statutory obligations.

This is not a universal rule requiring termination whenever the corporate structure changes. A merger, reorganisation or other case governed by special rules may require a different analysis. The legal and factual status of the employing entity must be established before deciding the paperwork.

## 3. When is an amendment to the original employment contract appropriate?

Where Company A remains the employer and the employee's work changes within that employment relationship, [Article 52.1](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751) may support an agreed amendment. The amendment could record a new project, location, reporting procedure, duties or remuneration, provided the arrangement is lawful and genuinely reflects the relationship.

However, an arrangement in which Company B directly supervises the employee as its own permanent staff member while Company A merely processes payroll may raise different legal questions. The actual facts should be tested against the employment relationship and labour-supply rules. A contract amendment alone is not a reliable way to avoid requirements that apply to a substantively different arrangement.

## 4. Labour supply is a regulated category, not a generic assignment

[Article 76 of the Labour Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751) addresses arrangements in which a labour-supply provider makes its employees available to another employer under a labour-supply agreement. The law specifies circumstances in which the receiving business may engage supplied employees, certain numerical limits and requirements for written or electronic contracting.

Article 77 addresses the rights and conditions of employees supplied under such arrangements. The [Law on Employment Promotion, Article 9¹](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/563), contains rules concerning registration of labour-supply service providers.

It is therefore unsafe to assume that two affiliated companies can describe any internal staffing move as an ordinary “assignment” and disregard those requirements. Equally, not every project involving employees of an affiliated service provider is necessarily regulated labour supply. **The functions, control and commercial substance** determine the questions that must be examined.

## 5. A management services agreement does not replace an employment contract

Group Company A may legitimately provide Company B with finance, HR, IT, management or other support services. The intercompany agreement can define service scope, outputs, fees, reporting, confidentiality, data protection, liability and the parties' commercial responsibilities.

However, the agreement between companies does not by itself change the identity of the legal employer of individual employees performing the services. Employment terms, workplace control and statutory employee rights must be addressed separately.

An individual employee amendment might clarify the project period, approved duties, additional remuneration, reporting lines, confidentiality and intellectual-property arrangements. It should not describe a transfer of full employer status unless the underlying legal transaction actually supports that outcome.

## 6. Settle payroll, leave and social insurance responsibilities

Before a permanent transfer, Company A should reconcile unpaid wages, earned incentives, overtime, annual leave and the return of entrusted assets. Company B should establish a lawful new remuneration and reporting arrangement and clarify any intended recognition of prior service for particular employment benefits.

If group payroll is administered centrally after the transfer, that internal arrangement should be reviewed against employment, tax and social insurance obligations. **An intercompany accounting entry is not a substitute for identifying the actual employer** and meeting its statutory duties.

The parties should also consider any management incentive plan, confidentiality undertaking, training-cost arrangement or continuing restriction that may affect the employee after departure. These should be assessed for their own lawful basis and enforceability rather than copied automatically into the new contract.

## 7. Protect employee information and business confidentiality

Changing the work location or legal employer does not give the receiving company unrestricted access to the employee's personal file or the former employer's client database, source code, financial information or trade secrets.

The lawful basis for transferring employee records should be examined under the [Law on Personal Data Protection](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16390288615991) and the [Labour Law's Articles 45–46](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751). The scope of information, authorised recipients, security measures and recordkeeping should be limited to what the arrangement requires.

The employer should also control access credentials and business materials. An employee's authority to access one group company's systems should not be assumed to extend automatically to those of another entity.

## 8. A workable documentation sequence

For a typical proposed transfer, the following sequence helps prevent contradictory agreements:

1. Identify the current employer, receiving entity and intended post-transfer employer.
2. Discuss the role, salary, conditions, reporting line and intended start date with the employee.
3. If the employer changes, confirm a lawful basis to end the former relationship and execute the new agreement.
4. If the employer remains unchanged, evaluate whether an amendment, genuine service arrangement or regulated labour supply rules apply.
5. Reconcile wages, leave, insurance reporting, information access and company property.
6. Record who gives daily instructions, assesses performance and bears the relevant employment obligations.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a parent company permanently transfer someone to a subsidiary without their consent?** A unilateral internal decision or intercompany services contract does not automatically replace the employee's agreement or statutory employment protections. Any change must be assessed under the applicable legal basis and actual employment terms.

**Can one executive manage two group companies?** Potentially, but separate issues arise concerning appointment authority, corporate governance, conflicts of interest, employment contracts and remuneration. The label “management services” does not resolve them all.

**Must all employment contracts be replaced when ownership changes?** Not merely because shares are sold. [Article 80.3 of the Labour Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230709635751) provides that changes in affiliation, type of ownership, legal form or management are not in themselves grounds for dismissal, unless law provides otherwise. A share sale and a move to a new legal employer are different situations.

## Conclusion

For transfers within a corporate group, the central question is **whether the identity of the employer changes**. If it does, lawful termination and a new employment agreement may be appropriate. If it does not, the parties should examine an employment amendment, a genuine management services arrangement and any potentially applicable labour-supply rules. The documentation should follow the real relationship, not attempt to redefine it through labels.
