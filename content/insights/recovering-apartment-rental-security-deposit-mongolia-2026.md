Tenants in Mongolia frequently transfer a payment described as a security deposit before moving into a rented apartment. Disputes arise when the landlord retains that payment after the tenancy ends. The description “deposit” does not itself determine whether the money is refundable or may be deducted. The agreement, purpose of payment, outstanding obligations and available evidence must be assessed together.

## Rent, advance payments and deposits are not interchangeable

Under Article 302.1 of Mongolia's [Civil Code](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=299), a residential landlord must provide possession of the dwelling and the tenant must pay the agreed rent. An additional payment may represent rent for the final month, an advance payment or security for contractual performance. The agreement and payment records should clarify its purpose.

Suppose the monthly rent is MNT 1 million and the tenant separately pays MNT 1 million as security. If the tenant returns the apartment and owes no relevant amounts, there may be a contractual basis to demand repayment. Proven arrears or tenant-caused damage may require a reconciliation under the agreement. The figures are illustrative and do not represent a statutory deposit amount applicable to every tenancy.

## Which deductions require evidence?

Unpaid rent, agreed utility charges and proven damage attributable to the tenant may be considered. The landlord should be able to explain the legal basis and amount of each deduction. Ordinary wear from normal occupation must not be confused with damage caused by improper use. The cost of replacing an ageing fixture cannot automatically be charged in full to the departing tenant.

Conversely, a tenant cannot avoid liability for damage simply by describing every defect as ordinary wear. The cause of the damage and reasonable cost of repair are relevant. A vague assertion that the apartment was damaged will usually be less persuasive than contemporaneous photographs, an inventory and reliable cost evidence.

## The importance of a handover record

The parties should document the apartment's condition at the outset, including floors, walls, plumbing, appliances and furniture. At departure, they should record meter readings, returned keys, outstanding payments and any disputed defects. A jointly signed handover report can make later disagreements much easier to resolve.

Where no report exists, correspondence, photographs, bank records and other documentation may still be relevant. The tenant should preserve the record of the original deposit transfer and any communication about its intended purpose.

## What if the landlord refuses repayment?

The tenant should first assemble the agreement, bank confirmation, handover evidence and account of any unpaid charges. A written demand should identify the original deposit, accepted deductions, disputed items and amount requested. If the landlord claims repair costs, the tenant can request an itemised explanation and supporting evidence.

If the parties cannot settle, a civil claim may be possible under the Civil Code. The competent court, limitation period and precise legal grounds depend on the claim. A claimant must show not only that the money was transferred, but why an obligation to repay has arisen and remains unperformed.
