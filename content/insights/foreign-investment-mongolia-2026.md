On 3 July 2026 Mongolia adopted substantial amendments to the Investment Law and to several related laws. The amendments are directed at clarifying the legal protection of investors, improving public services and the handling of investor complaints, and revising the terms on which stabilisation certificates are granted.

## What counts as a foreign-invested company

Under the revised definition, a legal entity established in Mongolia is a foreign-invested legal entity where foreign investors hold 25 per cent or more of its total issued shares and each foreign investor has invested more than USD 100,000 or the equivalent in tugriks.

Foreign ownership of shares in a Mongolian company does not therefore create this classification automatically. Both the level of shareholding and the size of each individual investor's contribution have to be checked together.

The law on state registration also now requires the state registration of a foreign-invested legal entity to be completed within five working days of receiving the complete set of documents.

## The principles governing dealings with investors are now set out in the law

The 2026 amendments write into the law the principles applying to investment relations.

They include fair and equal treatment of investors, non-discrimination, protection of investments and lawful income, maintaining an investment environment that is transparent, stable and predictable, and performing contractual obligations in good faith.

On their own, these principles are not a guarantee against every commercial or regulatory risk. Their significance is that they serve as the basis for interpreting the law in disputes concerning decisions of public bodies, investment agreements and the rights of investors.

## Conditions for expropriation and compensation clarified

The basic protection against the unlawful seizure of an investor's property remains.

Where property has to be taken in the public interest, it may be taken only under the procedure laid down by law and on terms of full compensation. Compensation is to be based on the market valuation at the time the property was taken, or at the time the investor and the public were notified of it.

For a foreign investor, this is an important provision when assessing the legal basis of state measures affecting the assets, shares and other property of an investment project.

## The right to transfer profits and dividends abroad is confirmed

An investor that has fully discharged its tax obligations in Mongolia may transfer abroad operating profits, dividends, royalties for the use of intellectual property, service fees, principal and interest on loans taken from abroad, and the assets due to it following liquidation.

The law also guarantees the right to convert funds into a freely convertible international currency of the investor's choice and transfer them.

The right to transfer funds does not, however, make the payment tax free. The tax consequences of dividends, interest, service fees and every other transaction need to be assessed separately.

## Stabilisation certificate thresholds revised

The 2026 amendments revise the investment thresholds for a certificate stabilising tax rates.

| Investment amount | Stabilisation period |
| --- | --- |
| MNT 20–50 billion in Ulaanbaatar | 10 years |
| MNT 10–50 billion outside the capital | 10 years |
| MNT 50–100 billion | 15 years |
| Above MNT 100 billion | 20 years |

A stabilisation certificate allows the rates of corporate income tax, customs duty, VAT and mineral resource royalties to be stabilised on the terms set out in the law.

The term and conditions of stabilisation certificates granted before the 2026 amendments are expressly preserved by the transitional provisions until those certificates expire.

## Foreign state-owned companies need an additional approval

Private foreign investors and foreign state-owned companies are not subject to the same rules.

A foreign state-owned legal entity acquiring 33 per cent or more of the total shares of a Mongolian legal entity operating in mining, banking and finance, media or telecommunications requires approval from the competent authority.

Whether a foreign state participates, directly or indirectly, in the ultimate ownership of the investor therefore has to be established at the earliest stage of a transaction. On a share acquisition in a mining company in particular, this check belongs before the contract is signed.

## A dedicated channel for investor complaints and public services

One feature of the 2026 amendments is that the body responsible for investment is given a clearer mandate not only to attract investment but to retain investments already made and to protect the rights and lawful interests of investors.

That body is now responsible for resolving investor complaints, advising on other public services, providing an electronic single-window service, and maintaining a consolidated database of investment information.

The significance for an investor is an additional route for resolving issues concerning the decisions and services of public bodies at an earlier stage, rather than only protecting its rights once a court or arbitration dispute has arisen.

## Structure, permits and tax need to be assessed together before investing

Foreign investment should not be reduced to a question of company registration.

Before the investment structure is settled, the following need to be assessed together: whether the sector is open to foreign investors, whether a licence is required, the shareholding structure, the minimum investment amount, whether funding is provided as equity or debt, and what tax arises on transferring profits and loan repayments abroad.

Where the investment is in mining, finance or another regulated sector, the sector-specific legislation applies alongside the Investment Law.

## Conclusion

The 2026 investment law reform is less the creation of an entirely new framework for foreign investment than a clarification of investor protection, public services, stabilisation and the mechanism for resolving complaints.

For a foreign investor, the essential preparation for a transaction is unchanged: establish the corporate structure and the amount of the investment correctly before state registration, check the licensing requirements for the sector, and work out the tax and repatriation position in advance.
