An **IT and technology lawyer in Mongolia** needs to understand more than the wording of a commercial agreement. Software release processes, API integrations, SaaS uptime metrics, source-code ownership and audit logs often determine what can be proved in a dispute. Legal advice is more useful when it connects the contract to the way the system actually operates.

## 1. Software development: what counts as delivery and acceptance?

A deployed application is not necessarily the same as full contractual performance. The relevant records may include the statement of work, technical specifications, acceptance criteria, test evidence, defect history, security requirements, repository access and deployment documentation.

A customer might complain that the developer did not hand over source code while the supplier replies that the system is live. Those positions need to be examined by separating the functional deliverable, source-code access, relevant licences, administrator credentials and technical documentation.

## 2. Copyright: separate the author from the holder of exploitation rights

Article 22 of Mongolia's [Law on Copyright](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16230812154721) contains specific rules for computer programs. Under Article 22.3, the exclusive right to exploit commissioned software belongs to the customer unless the agreement provides otherwise. That does not automatically assign ownership of pre-existing libraries, third-party commercial software or every open-source component used in the project.

A sound assessment requires the actual contract, bill of materials or dependencies, licence terms and project history. Possessing a copy of code, being identified as the author and holding the economic right to exploit the commissioned work are not necessarily identical legal positions.

## 3. SaaS and service-level agreements: proving an outage

SaaS contracts may specify uptime targets, permitted maintenance windows, incident response, backups, service credits, termination rights and reporting requirements. In a dispute, server monitoring, incident tickets, agreed calculation methods and the timing of notices may be more useful than an isolated screenshot.

For illustration, if an SLA promises 99.5% uptime, the relevant inquiry is how the contract defines the measurement window, exclusions and data source. That percentage is an example, not a claim about LawMongolia's standard terms or a reported case.

## 4. API integrations: distinguishing system failure from contractual responsibility

API disputes may arise from duplicate calls, inaccurate address or tariff data, missed webhooks, upstream provider outages or authentication failures. Request IDs, timestamps, HTTP responses, payload versions, retry behaviour, integration specifications and audit trails help identify what happened.

The legal assessment should then examine any API-call billing formula, access limits, service levels, key-management obligations and the allocation of third-party dependency risks. The mere fact that one service was unavailable does not identify which contracting party bears liability.

## 5. Personal data and cyber incidents

The [Law on Personal Data Protection](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16390288615991) addresses legal grounds for collection and processing, data-subject rights and controller/processor responsibilities. The [Law on Cybersecurity](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/16390365491061) may impose additional obligations depending on the organisation's role and operations.

In a suspected data incident, preservation of access records, permission changes, cloud-audit logs and a reliable incident timeline may be essential. A confidentiality clause alone does not replace statutory data-protection duties.

## 6. When does a company need an advocate and when a technical expert?

Engineers and forensic specialists analyse the software and incident facts. A lawyer or licensed advocate links those facts to the contract, available remedies, evidentiary procedure and litigation strategy. Neither professional category should be assumed to replace the other. Complex matters may require both.

## Evidence checklist for an IT dispute

- Master service agreement, statement of work and approved specification;
- Acceptance records, test results and defect tracker;
- Repository permissions, commit history and relevant version logs;
- SaaS uptime monitoring, support tickets and incident chronology;
- API request IDs, response records and integration documentation;
- Data-processing arrangements, access-control and permission logs;
- Invoices, payment history and reconciliation records;
- Notices of breach, demands and negotiation correspondence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a technology lawyer handle only intellectual-property disputes?** No. Work may extend to development contracts, SaaS availability, API billing, data processing, platform liability and related litigation.

**Must an advocate personally audit source code?** Not necessarily. The legal task is to identify which technical facts require proof and coordinate appropriate expert evidence where needed.
