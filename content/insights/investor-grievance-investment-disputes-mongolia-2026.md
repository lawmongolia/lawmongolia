Amendments to Mongolia’s Investment Law adopted on **3 July 2026** introduced a more structured legal framework for protecting investors through both an administrative grievance mechanism and a dedicated state-investor dispute-resolution process.

The amendments also added express principles requiring fair and equitable treatment of investors, non-discrimination, protection of investment and lawfully earned proceeds, transparency, stability and predictability of the investment environment, good-faith performance of contractual obligations, and support for sustainable and responsible investment.

These principles provide more than a general investment-policy statement. They form part of the statutory framework against which government conduct and investor grievances may be assessed.

## An investor grievance is distinct from an investment dispute

The amended law distinguishes an “investor grievance” from an “investment dispute”.

An investor may submit a grievance to the state administrative body responsible for investment where an unlawful act or omission of a government body or official has affected the investor’s rights or legitimate interests. An investment dispute, by contrast, concerns a legal dispute between an investor and a Mongolian state body arising from an investment or investment activity.

The distinction is important. The grievance mechanism is intended to provide an administrative route for addressing government conduct before or outside formal litigation, while an investment dispute is governed by contractual, arbitration or judicial dispute-resolution mechanisms.

## A 30-day administrative review period

The investment authority may obtain explanations and information from the relevant government body when reviewing a grievance. The relevant body or official must provide a reasoned response within **five days**.

The grievance is generally to be reviewed within **30 days**, with a possible extension of a further 30 days where necessary. Where wrongful conduct is identified, the investment authority may issue proposals and conclusions to the relevant government body or its superior authority and may, where appropriate, refer the matter to the Government for consideration.

The reform therefore gives investors a dedicated administrative channel for escalating investment-related problems across government institutions.

## The grievance mechanism has jurisdictional limits

The investment authority may refuse grievances concerning matters already pending before or decided by the Constitutional Court, courts or investigative authorities; matters pending before or decided by domestic or foreign arbitration or a foreign court; certain national-security and foreign-policy matters; matters under review by the National Human Rights Commission; and tax assessments falling within the jurisdiction of the Tax Dispute Resolution Council.

The mechanism is therefore not a universal appeal body replacing specialised judicial, arbitral or tax procedures.

## Filing a grievance does not waive court or arbitration rights

Article 23.10 expressly provides that resolution of an investor grievance by the investment authority **does not restrict the investor’s right to pursue the matter through a court or arbitration**.

This provides investors with an important strategic option: an attempt to resolve a government-related issue administratively does not, by itself, constitute a waiver of subsequent legal remedies.

However, where proceedings have already moved into court or arbitration, the investment authority may decline to accept the grievance under the statutory exclusions. The sequencing of remedies should therefore be planned carefully.

## The investment agreement takes priority in state-investor disputes

New Article 24 provides that, unless Mongolian legislation otherwise requires, an investment dispute between an investor and a Mongolian state body is to be resolved in accordance with the applicable **investment agreement**.

Where no investment agreement exists, or the agreement does not address the dispute, the parties are to pursue negotiation or mediation. If the dispute cannot be resolved through those methods within **six months**, it may be submitted to a domestic or international arbitration mutually agreed by the parties. Matters placed by law within the exclusive jurisdiction of Mongolian courts must be resolved by the Mongolian courts.

This significantly increases the importance of properly drafted dispute-resolution clauses in investment agreements.

## Dispute clauses should be negotiated before a dispute exists

Once a dispute has arisen, it may be difficult for an investor and a state body to agree on an arbitral institution, seat, procedural rules or language.

Investment agreements should therefore address when a dispute notice is deemed received, when the negotiation period begins, whether mediation is mandatory or optional, the arbitral institution, seat, rules, language, number of arbitrators, interim relief and enforcement arrangements.

Because Article 24.2 refers to arbitration “mutually agreed” by the parties, documenting the arbitration agreement in advance provides materially greater legal certainty than leaving that negotiation until after the dispute has crystallised.

## Applicable investment treaties must also be reviewed

Article 2.2 of the Investment Law continues to provide that where an international treaty of Mongolia contains rules different from the Law, the international treaty applies.

Accordingly, a foreign-investment dispute should not be analysed solely under Article 24 of the domestic Investment Law. The investor’s nationality, any applicable bilateral or multilateral investment treaty and the contractual dispute-resolution provisions should all be reviewed together.

## Conclusion

The practical significance of the 2026 amendments is that investor protection is moving from general statutory assurances toward a **structured system of administrative grievance review and legal dispute resolution**.

Investors now have a dedicated avenue to raise unlawful government conduct without automatically forfeiting court or arbitration remedies. At the same time, the amendments make careful drafting of investment agreements, escalation procedures and arbitration clauses materially more important.
