An employee may be held financially liable where, through the employee's fault, the employee causes property damage to the employer while performing work duties. However, the existence of a business loss does not give an employer an unrestricted right to deduct any amount from the employee's salary.

## Limited liability is the general rule

As a general rule, an employee's limited financial liability may not exceed one month's average salary, unless one of the special statutory rules on greater or full liability applies.

## When full liability applies

Full financial liability applies only in the circumstances specifically identified by law. These include, among others, certain criminal conduct established by a final court judgment, damage caused by an employee subject to a valid full-liability arrangement, failure to account for entrusted property, loss of specifically entrusted equipment, and certain damage caused while intoxicated.

Outside the statutory cases, full financial liability may not be imposed.

## Establishing the amount of damage

Before determining the amount of damage, the employer must examine the circumstances. The employee may be required to provide a written or electronic explanation.

The amount is based on actual property damage. Expected or lost profit is not included, and depreciation must be taken into account.

## Ceilings on deductions

Where an employer makes a lawful salary deduction, the total deduction in a month generally may not exceed 20 per cent of the employee's salary after social insurance, health insurance and personal income tax deductions. The ceiling is 50 per cent in certain cases involving multiple deductions or child support. The employee must be notified in advance.

## Amounts above one month's salary

If the employer seeks compensation exceeding one month's average salary, the Labour Law requires the employer to pursue the claim through court rather than deducting the entire amount from salary by unilateral decision.

An employee who disagrees with a deduction or its amount may challenge it through the labour rights dispute resolution procedure.

## Conclusion

Before recovering damage from an employee, the employer must establish the employee's fault, the actual damage suffered, its amount, and which type of financial liability applies. Deducting an entire loss from salary because stock is missing, goods were damaged or the company suffered a shortfall does not comply with the Labour Law.
