Companies using token-based incentive structures — particularly in technology and fintech — increasingly rely on concepts such as vesting schedules, cliff periods and token grant agreements as part of employee compensation. The question is what legal risks arise when such agreements are applied directly to an employment relationship governed by Mongolian law.

## A token grant agreement cannot replace an employment contract

The most common mistake is a company relying solely on a token vesting agreement, without a written employment contract that meets the requirements of the Labour Law.

Article 24 of the Labour Law requires the employer to conclude a written employment contract with the employee, specifying mandatory terms including position, remuneration, working hours, leave and grounds for termination.

A token vesting agreement may serve as an additional arrangement governing equity or digital-asset compensation, but it cannot substitute for the underlying employment contract. Both agreements should exist together, and each must independently comply with Mongolian law.

## Are tokens or equity compensation treated as salary

Whether a vesting benefit is treated as part of salary or as a separate capital-type benefit matters significantly for tax and social insurance purposes.

Where the benefit is directly tied to the employee's performance and delivered on a recurring basis, the tax authority may treat it as employment income, triggering personal income tax and social insurance contributions.

The tax characterisation of the benefit should therefore be determined at the outset, in consultation with tax advisers, before the vesting structure is implemented.

## What happens to unvested tokens on termination

Foreign template agreements commonly state that unvested tokens are forfeited upon termination. This needs to be reconciled with Mongolian labour law principles.

Where an employee leaves or is dismissed, the Labour Law requires the employer to settle salary and other amounts accrued for the period actually worked. If the vesting benefit is treated as part of salary, the portion attributable to the period worked may need to be paid in full.

Where the benefit is instead structured as a conditional incentive, separate from performance of work and contingent on meeting future conditions, a different conclusion may apply. The distinction should be drawn clearly in the agreement.

## Practical recommendations

Before implementing a token-based incentive structure, ensure the underlying employment contract is fully compliant with Mongolian law, clearly characterise whether the vesting benefit is salary or a separate capital-type incentive, determine its tax treatment in advance, and draft the termination and forfeiture provisions so that they do not conflict with the Labour Law.

## Conclusion

Applying a foreign token vesting template directly and without modification to an employment relationship in Mongolia carries risk on both the labour-law and tax sides. Before introducing such an incentive structure, the underlying employment contract should be put in place separately and made fully compliant with Mongolian law.
