Unemployment benefits in Mongolia are payments made from the unemployment insurance fund to insured persons who become unemployed and satisfy the statutory conditions. Losing a job is not sufficient by itself. Eligibility depends on the unemployment insurance contribution record, registration with the relevant employment authority, active job seeking and the timing of the benefit application. Amendments enacted in July 2026 also introduce changes taking effect on 1 January 2027, making it important to distinguish the rules applicable in each period.

Articles 9–15 of the [Law on Benefits from the Social Insurance Fund](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760148705641) govern eligibility, calculation, payment, application deadlines and decision-making. The provisions and effective-date notes should be checked when a claim involves a change in employment status around the end of 2026.

## Eligibility requirements in 2026

Under Article 9.1 as applicable in 2026, the insured person must have contributed to unemployment insurance for at least 24 months in total, including the last nine consecutive months before becoming unemployed. The person must be registered with the responsible employment body, actively seeking work and below the applicable ordinary retirement age.

An employee who has worked for several years but did not make the required unemployment insurance contributions during the most recent nine-month period may therefore fail the continuity requirement. Actual contribution records, rather than a broad statement of employment history, are relevant to the decision.

Article 9.2 separately addresses a claimant who has previously received unemployment benefits. Under the provision applicable in 2026, eligibility may arise again following a further 12 months of contributions, including the last three consecutive months before unemployment. This continuity requirement is scheduled to change in 2027.

Article 9.4 excludes certain categories of termination, including departure to undertake compulsory military service and specified grounds related to criminal sentences or retirement. Article 9.3 contains a special rule for an insured mother whose employer is liquidated or declared insolvent during childcare leave or within the specified period after returning to work.

## Benefit rates and illustrative calculation

For 2026, Article 10.1 uses average salary or equivalent income subject to contributions during the last nine consecutive months of contributions before unemployment. The applicable rate is determined by total months of unemployment insurance contributions. The rate is 50 percent for 24–59 months, 55 percent for 60–119 months, 60 percent for 120–179 months and 70 percent for 180 months or more.

Consider an employee with eight years of contributions and an average monthly contributory income of MNT 2,100,000 during the relevant period. The 55 percent rate applies. Using the statutory daily average of monthly income divided by 21, the daily income base is MNT 100,000 and the illustrative daily benefit is MNT 55,000. Multiplying this by 76 eligible working days gives MNT 4,180,000. This is an explanatory example rather than an official award: the insurer must verify the contribution record, relevant days and all other applicable conditions.

The statutory rate is not an amount payable automatically for every calendar month. Entitlement and payment days are determined under the governing rules, and the benefit is paid through the insured person's bank account.

## Payment period and filing deadline in 2026

Article 11.1 provides a standard benefit period of 76 working days beginning the day after dismissal, under the rules applicable in 2026. Article 14.1.3 requires the application to be made within four weeks from the employer's decision ending employment, or within three months where an acceptable reason for delay exists. The application may be made on paper or electronically.

A claimant should promptly confirm registration as an active job seeker, obtain the termination decision and check the social insurance contribution statement. Where the filing is made after the ordinary deadline, Article 11.3 permits the deduction of late working days from the benefit period in the circumstances specified by law. Under Article 15.1, the social insurance authority must decide a properly documented unemployment benefit claim within 14 days after receipt.

Payment may cease if the claimant starts a new job and resumes mandatory contributions, is awarded a qualifying pension or another statutory stopping ground applies. Article 11.4 also deals with a person who returns to work and is again dismissed within five months, providing for certain unpaid benefits remaining from the original entitlement.

## Changes scheduled for 1 January 2027

The amendments adopted on 2 July 2026 introduce a requirement for contributions during the last 12 consecutive months before unemployment, rather than nine months. The income period used for calculating the benefit also changes from the last nine consecutive contribution months to the last 12. A previous benefit recipient seeking a renewed entitlement must meet a revised continuity condition involving the last nine months of the further contribution period.

Most importantly, the amended Article 11.1 differentiates the payment period by the ground of departure. The general 76-working-day period remains, but a person who resigns voluntarily will receive benefits calculated for 55 working days under the provision taking effect on 1 January 2027. This should not be represented as a rule already governing all resignations in October 2026.

Accurate eligibility decisions require the social insurance record, the employer's termination decision, employment registration and dates of the claim. Individual calculations and administrative decisions should be checked against the law applicable to the particular entitlement.
