If a neighbor's wrongful conduct damages your property, someone breaks your laptop, or another person causes damage to your vehicle, what must the responsible person pay? Does the law require a brand-new replacement, the cost of repair, or compensation in money? Where damage is caused outside a contractual obligation, the issue is generally considered under the Civil Code rules governing **liability for harm (tort)** rather than under the rules on defective goods or breach of a sales contract.

## What is a tort, and who is liable?

[Article 497.1 of Mongolia's Civil Code](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=299) addresses unlawful intentional or negligent acts or omissions that cause harm to another person's protected rights or interests, including property. This article focuses on property damage. The legal analysis ordinarily concerns the wrongful act or omission, the loss, causal connection and the relevant rules on fault.

**Article 497.2** provides that a person who caused harm may be exempt from liability, except where the law provides otherwise, if that person proves the damage was not attributable to his or her fault. This does not mean that a victim must first obtain a criminal conviction before bringing a civil claim. Civil, criminal and administrative-offence liability may have different legal requirements and evidentiary questions.

Special provisions may apply to activities involving particular risks, such as the use of motor vehicles under **Article 499**. In a vehicle-damage dispute it may be necessary to identify not only the driver but also the owner, possessor and actual circumstances of the vehicle's use. Article 497 should not be treated as the only potentially applicable basis in every property-damage dispute.

## Repair, replacement or monetary compensation?

Under **Article 510.1**, a person responsible for damage to another's property must remedy the harm by restoring the prior position, such as supplying property of the same kind and quality or repairing the damaged item, **or by compensating the damage in money**. The purpose is to address the proven loss, not to grant an automatic entitlement to the latest model of a product simply because an older item was damaged.

Suppose an older laptop has been damaged and a technician estimates that restoring it to working order will cost MNT 450,000. The repair quotation, diagnosis and any payment records may be important evidence where repair can genuinely restore the item to its previous condition. If an item is destroyed beyond repair, determining compensation requires an assessment of the actual property, its condition, age, value and other proven circumstances. ## What evidence supports the amount claimed?

A victim should document the property's condition, the damage and the circumstances of the incident as promptly as practicable. Depending on the case, useful records include photographs, video, an incident report, witnesses, purchase documents, technical assessments, professional valuations, repair quotations and receipts. No single document is automatically conclusive in every case.

Under [Article 38.1 of the Civil Procedure Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=302), parties must produce and collect evidence supporting the factual basis of their claims and defenses. **Articles 38.3 and 38.6** permit court assistance with evidence in specified circumstances, but a claimant should not expect the court to estimate the loss without a substantiated basis. A repair quotation alone may be questioned if its necessity, relationship to the incident or underlying methodology has not been established.

## Can the victim's conduct affect compensation?

**Article 514.1** allows the court to take account of the victim's gross negligence where it contributed to the occurrence of harm or an increase in the resulting damage. The mere fact that someone suffered a loss does not establish fault on the victim's part. A specific act or omission and its causal contribution must be examined. The result is fact-sensitive and cannot be expressed as an automatic fixed percentage reduction.

## Why is the limitation period five years?

**Article 75.2.4** establishes a **five-year limitation period for claims arising from harm to another person's property**. This differs from the generally applicable special three-year limitation for contractual claims. Properly identifying whether a dispute arises from breach of contract or an independent tort is therefore essential before deciding which deadline governs.

Under **Articles 76.1 and 76.2**, the limitation period runs from the emergence of the right to demand performance, taking account, unless otherwise specified by law, of the infringement of a right and when the claimant knew or ought to have known of it. It would be unsafe to say that the five-year clock invariably starts on the exact date of an accident. The proper starting date, nature of the claim and any applicable suspension or interruption rules must be assessed on the facts. Nor should this property-damage limitation be automatically extended to all claims concerning personal injury, life or reputation.

## Demand letter or court action?

A written demand identifying the incident, the alleged harm, the required repair or payment, supporting documents and a reasonable response opportunity can help resolve the dispute or record the opposing party's position. Negotiations, however, should not be treated as a substitute for checking limitation periods. If the matter remains unresolved, a civil claim must correctly identify the liable party and establish the damage, causal relationship and amount claimed with admissible evidence.

Where a traffic accident, insurance contract or another specially regulated activity is involved, the relevant special statutes, policy terms and claims procedure must also be examined. General tort principles do not displace every sector-specific rule.
