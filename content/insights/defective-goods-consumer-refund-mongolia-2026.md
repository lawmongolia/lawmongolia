A retailer may tell a customer that an appliance, phone or piece of furniture can be repaired but never returned for a refund. Internal store policy, however, does not displace statutory rights concerning defective goods. The appropriate remedy depends on the defect, its cause, any applicable warranty and the sale contract.

## Remedies for defective goods

Article 6.3 of Mongolia's [Law on Consumer Protection](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=551) provides several potential remedies where goods are defective, incomplete or fail to meet quality and quantity requirements. They include free repair, reimbursement of repair costs, a proportionate price reduction, replacement with conforming goods and, in applicable circumstances, return of the goods and price or cancellation of the contract.

These are not necessarily concurrent or unconditional remedies. The nature of the defect, the possibility of repair, its impact on the agreed use and whether consumer misuse caused the problem can influence the available claim.

## Does an expired warranty defeat every claim?

Article 6.3.7 addresses latent defects discovered within the warranty period that were not caused by the consumer. Depending on the legal conditions, replacement, free repair or repayment may be available. When the warranty has expired, the Civil Code provisions concerning defects in sold goods and the periods for bringing claims should also be examined. A “no warranty” notice does not automatically eliminate every legal protection.

However, proven misuse, unauthorised repair or accidental damage may change the analysis. Establishing the origin of the problem is often central to the dispute.

## Does a no-returns sign end the matter?

Returning goods merely because a purchaser changes their mind is different from seeking a statutory remedy because the goods are defective. A retailer's no-returns sign should not be mistaken for an answer to every defective-goods claim. Equally, the law should not be described as allowing unrestricted returns of all fault-free purchases for any reason.

## What evidence matters?

Preserve receipts, bank confirmations, model and serial numbers, warranty documents, online order records and retailer correspondence. Photograph or record the defect as soon as possible. A technical examination or specialist report may be necessary if the cause is disputed.

Present a specific written request for repair, replacement, a price adjustment or repayment. Explain the defect, the remedy sought and the available evidence. If the retailer refuses, seek a written explanation and preserve it for any further proceedings.

## Can the consumer recover additional loss?

Articles 6.1 and 6.2 of the Consumer Protection Law address harm and compensation. The claimant must establish the loss, its amount and its causal connection to the defect or breach. A defective product does not entitle a purchaser to an unlimited sum without supporting evidence.
