An old-age pension in Mongolia is not necessarily equal to the salary a person received before retiring. Entitlement and payment depend on the applicable retirement age, months of pension insurance contributions, the salary base used by the social insurance system, the calculation method and the law in force at the relevant time. Amendments adopted on 2 July 2026 include provisions that will take effect from 2027 and be implemented in stages.

Articles 5–8, 10 and 20 of the [Law on Pensions from the Social Insurance Fund](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760148505951) govern the principal conditions. Where a provision includes a future commencement annotation, it must not be applied as though it were already in force in October 2026.

## Pensionable age is not the same for every insured person

Article 5 distinguishes ordinary retirement, retirement based on the relevant contribution period and special entitlements concerning certain mothers and workers in specified arduous or hazardous conditions. The ordinary retirement age depends on the person's year of birth. It was historically 60 for men born in or before 1957 and 55 for women born in or before 1962, and the statutory schedule generally increases the relevant age by three months for later birth years until the eventual age of 65.

Consequently, it is inaccurate to advise every man to retire at 60, every woman at 55 or every insured person at 65 without examining the date of birth and the specific legal route to a pension. Articles 5.1 and 5.2 also distinguish full and proportionate pension entitlement by contribution period. Special rules for insured persons who have raised the required number of children and those working in qualifying conditions must be examined separately.

The contribution period specified for certain retirement routes is subject to transitional increases. An applicant should therefore check the exact contribution months recognised by the social insurance register and the threshold applicable in the year the entitlement is assessed.

## The wage-based method and an illustrative pension calculation

Article 6 distinguishes the wage-based and accumulation-based pension calculation methods. Which method applies depends in part on the insured person's birth year and statutory exceptions. For the wage-based method, Article 7.1.1 provides a full-pension formula starting at **45 percent of the relevant average monthly earnings**, with an additional **0.125 percent of that earnings base for each contribution month above 240 months**.

This is equivalent to an additional 1.5 percentage points for each complete contribution year beyond 20 years. The percentage is applied to the legally determined pensionable salary base, not simply to the person's last take-home monthly salary.

For illustration, suppose the wage-based method applies to an insured person with 25 years, or 300 months, of pension contributions and a legally determined pensionable average monthly income of MNT 2,000,000. Sixty months exceed the first 240. The illustrative percentage is therefore 45 percent plus 60 times 0.125 percent, or 52.5 percent. The corresponding illustrative monthly pension is MNT 1,050,000. Actual calculation requires confirmation of the contribution record, earnings adjustments, statutory minimums and any other relevant provisions.

Article 7.1.2 contains a separate formula for a proportionate pension. The same average salary does not necessarily yield the same pension where the recognised periods of contributions differ.

## Which years of salary are used?

Under the wage-based rules applicable in 2026, the relevant salary reference generally consists of a consecutive **five-year period** within the specified recent contribution history. The amount is based on income subject to pension contributions, adjusted according to the legally applicable coefficients. It should not be confused with the employee's net salary deposited into a bank account.

The July 2026 amendments revise Article 20.1 towards a longer period, ultimately **ten years**, but Article 20.5 provides for gradual implementation. The reference period remains **five years in 2027 and 2028**, becomes **six years in 2029**, **seven years in 2030**, **eight years in 2031**, **nine years in 2032**, and **ten years from 2033**. The statutory rules also specify how the relevant monthly totals are divided in these transitional years. The new ten-year period should therefore not be represented as applying in full from 1 January 2027.

Articles 7.4 and 7.5 further provide for changes to pension calculations and post-retirement contribution increases from 2029. Those future percentage rules are not part of a calculation made under the 2026 regime.

## Application date and payment commencement

Article 10.1 distinguishes an application submitted before the insured person reaches the relevant retirement age from one submitted afterwards. In the first case, entitlement to payment begins from the date the relevant age is reached; where the application is submitted after that date, payment generally commences when the social insurance authority receives the application. Delaying the application can therefore affect the starting date of payment.

Before submitting an application, a prospective pensioner should review recorded contribution months, declared insurable earnings, employment under qualifying conditions, any special entitlement based on raising children and supporting historical documents. Discrepancies in the contribution record should be addressed with the relevant social insurance body and supporting evidence before the award is finalised.
