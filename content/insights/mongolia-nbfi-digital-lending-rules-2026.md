On 9 July 2026, the Financial Regulatory Commission adopted a new **Regulation on Non-Bank Financial Lending Activities under Resolution No. 278**, replacing the previous framework adopted under Resolution No. 71 of 2025. The regulation addresses both conventional lending and lending delivered through advanced digital technology.

The regulatory significance goes well beyond a debt-to-income cap. The framework now connects credit underwriting, automated system settings, customer identification, real-time credit reporting, third-party technology and cybersecurity obligations within a single regulatory structure.

## A 55% debt-to-income ceiling for consumer lending

Consumer loans issued by NBFIs must generally comply with a **maximum debt-to-income ratio of 55%**. Consumer loans secured by, or used to purchase, passenger vehicles are also subject to a maximum loan-to-value ratio of 60%. Green and social loans falling within the applicable sustainable-finance taxonomy are excluded from these limits.

For digital lending, the 55% requirement must also be embedded into the system itself: the customer’s available credit must be calculated automatically by reference to the regulatory ratio.

Regulatory compliance therefore becomes part of system configuration rather than merely a provision in an internal credit policy.

## Prior notification and minimum technology standards

An NBFI must notify the Financial Regulatory Commission at least **10 working days before launching** a digital lending product using advanced technology.

The institution must also satisfy ISO/IEC 27001 or an equivalent information-security standard and maintain paid-in capital of at least **MNT 5 billion**.

Digital lending is therefore treated as a distinct regulated delivery model, not simply as an ordinary loan product offered through a mobile interface.

## Biometric verification for each digital loan

For each digital loan, the NBFI must, with the data subject’s consent, use biometric information other than fingerprints to identify and authenticate the customer.

This creates a higher identification threshold than an onboarding model based solely on passwords, devices or telephone numbers.

Because biometric information is sensitive personal information, the process should also be assessed under applicable data-protection requirements, including the legal basis for collection, purpose limitation, retention, security and any processing undertaken by third-party providers.

## A 24-hour delay after multiple recent credit enquiries

One of the most notable controls in the 2026 framework applies where a borrower has sought credit from multiple institutions within a short period.

If the borrower’s credit report shows that **five or more banks or financial institutions have reviewed the borrower for lending within the previous 72 hours**, the NBFI must delay its credit review by **24 hours**, obtain an updated credit report and only then decide whether to lend.

The rule is designed to reduce the risk of a customer obtaining several loans in rapid succession before all new liabilities are reflected in the credit-information system.

For digital lenders, this needs to operate as an automated control in the credit-decision workflow.

## Real-time credit information

Digital lending and every subsequent change to relevant loan information must be reported to the credit-information database in **real time**. Where a revolving or credit-line facility is provided, the debt-to-income ratio must be recalculated within intervals not exceeding three months and the available credit limit updated accordingly.

Credit origination and credit reporting therefore operate as connected components of the same regulated process.

## Outsourcing technology does not outsource regulatory responsibility

An NBFI may use a third-party information-technology platform or data-processing system. However, where lending decisions rely on information processed through that third-party system, the NBFI must provide prior notification to the Financial Regulatory Commission.

The relevant technology provider must satisfy applicable Cybersecurity Law requirements, while the NBFI remains responsible for specified cybersecurity obligations and must report measures taken in response to relevant findings, recommendations and requirements by **31 March of the following year**.

Outsourcing an application, scoring engine or identity-verification platform therefore does not transfer the regulated institution’s compliance responsibility to the vendor.

## Transparency toward borrowers

Where an NBFI rejects a loan application, it must provide the customer with a reasoned explanation.

Loan advertising must also state the interest rate and general product conditions. If service fees and charges are set above the previous year’s industry average, the Financial Regulatory Commission may formally propose a reduction, with a defined response process for the NBFI.

The framework therefore combines speed and automation with stronger requirements for transparency and consumer protection.

## Conclusion

Mongolia’s 2026 regulation treats digital lending as the intersection of **finance, software, personal data, cybersecurity and consumer protection**.

For NBFIs, compliance cannot be achieved by updating loan agreements and internal policies alone. Credit algorithms, customer authentication, credit-database interfaces, vendor agreements, information-security controls and customer communications all need to be assessed as parts of one regulated lending system.
