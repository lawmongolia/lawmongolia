A lawsuit, regulatory appeal or shareholder dispute may force a business to select counsel quickly. Yet reputation and confident predictions are not sufficient grounds for appointment. Commercial claims, employment litigation, administrative appeals and criminal proceedings involve different procedural rules, evidentiary questions, deadlines and costs. The starting point should be how a prospective lawyer analyses the actual dispute, including its weaknesses.

## 1. Verify the right to practise as an advocate

Under Article 7.1 of Mongolia's [Law on Advocacy](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14859), an individual must meet the statutory examination and registration requirements to exercise the regulated right to practise advocacy. Describing oneself as a lawyer, consultant or contract reviewer does not by itself prove authorisation to act as an advocate in court. Verify the individual's current status and any restrictions applicable to the engagement.

## 2. Ask for relevant rather than generic experience

A lawyer who regularly works on complex commercial contracts may not necessarily handle criminal defence, and a capable trial advocate may have limited familiarity with fintech regulation. Ask about the kinds of claims, applications and evidentiary issues the lawyer has handled. A lawyer can usually explain their approach without identifying confidential clients or disclosing protected case facts. Also determine whether the same lawyer will work on an appeal if one becomes necessary.

## 3. Obtain a preliminary case assessment

An assessment should identify the legal cause of action or defence, jurisdiction, limitation periods where relevant, gaps in evidence, prospects of settlement, cost exposure and the practical ability to enforce a judgment. A favourable decision may still have limited economic value if recovery is unrealistic. Counsel should distinguish facts they have verified from assumptions supplied by the client, and explain uncertainties rather than promise a win.

## 4. Check conflicts of interest before providing sensitive information

A law firm may already advise an opposing party or hold confidential information connected to the dispute. Addressing conflicts early protects the parties, the integrity of the representation and the information shared. The Law on Advocacy contains specific duties concerning professional secrecy, independence and conflicts; these should be assessed before a contentious mandate is accepted.

## 5. Sign a clear written or electronic engagement

Article 22.1 of the Law on Advocacy requires a professional legal-assistance agreement in writing or electronic form. Article 22.2 identifies mandatory content, including the work, fees and payment terms, expenses, liabilities and termination. The document should state whether the mandate covers the claim or defence, interim applications, hearings, appeal proceedings or enforcement. A bank transfer or a brief exchange of chat messages is not a substitute for defining the mandate properly.

## 6. Separate legal fees from procedural expenses

Under Article 23.1, legal fees are determined by agreement. Fixed fees, stage-based fees and time-based billing may all be commercially appropriate, depending on the work. However, court fees, expert fees, translation costs, travel and other disbursements should be identified separately, as should any approval needed before substantial additional work is undertaken. No advocate can legitimately guarantee a judicial outcome: the result depends on the law, the evidence and the court.

## 7. Plan how evidence will be preserved

Commercial litigation often depends on original agreements, payment records, emails, platform logs, meeting records, delivery documents and proof that notices were served. Put these materials in chronological order and identify which disputed fact each item may establish. Screenshots alone may be insufficient to establish authenticity, completeness or origin in a contested case. Consider early preservation of underlying files and electronic records, rather than waiting until litigation is well advanced.

## 8. Agree reporting arrangements and decision authority

Who receives court notices? How quickly must counsel forward them? Who may approve settlement terms or changes to the claim? What powers should be included in a procedural authorisation? A practical reporting protocol helps the client respond before procedural opportunities expire. The lawyer should advise on options and risks, while material settlement and commercial decisions remain subject to the client's lawful authority.

## Conclusion

Selecting an advocate in Mongolia is not merely a search for someone with an impressive biography. Verify professional standing, assess fit for the dispute, insist on a reasoned case assessment and document scope, fees, evidence arrangements and authority. These steps do not ensure success, but they substantially improve the quality and accountability of legal representation.
