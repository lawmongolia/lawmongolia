Artificial intelligence is increasingly capable of supporting public services, risk assessments, regulatory reviews and administrative decision-making. In 2026, Mongolia introduced more specific statutory rules governing the use of AI in public-sector information systems.

Amendments dated 3 July 2026 added statutory definitions of an "AI-enabled system" and an "automated decision" to the Law on Transparency of Public Information, and introduced a new article 29¹ governing the use of AI.

## This is not a general AI act for the private sector

These provisions are not a general AI Act applicable to every private company.

The law applies to defined public information controllers: state bodies, state- and locally-owned or partially owned entities, entities exercising specified public functions under law or contract, and certain other bodies listed by the statute.

## AI outputs must be capable of human verification

Article 29¹.2 sets out several basic requirements for a public information controller using an AI system.

Its outputs must be capable of human verification. The system must retain information on the processing, the sources of the data used, the outputs and the access history. Measures must be in place to prevent discriminatory outcomes for individuals and legal entities. And where a result was based on inaccurate or incomplete information, it must be capable of being reprocessed.

The direction of the rule is towards an audit trail: a public system using AI must retain not only the final answer but a record of how that answer came about.

## AI may not finalise a decision affecting rights

The most significant safeguard is in article 29¹.3.

Where AI or an automated system is used to make a decision affecting the rights or legitimate interests of a person or legal entity, an authorised official must review and confirm that decision.

An algorithm may therefore produce a risk assessment, a recommendation or a draft. Human review is required on the final decision that actually affects rights.

## The use of AI must be disclosed

Where an AI system has been used to generate a recommendation, conclusion, response, report or decision, the document must carry an indication that AI was used.

This allows a citizen or company receiving a document from the state to know whether it was prepared independently by a person or with the involvement of AI.

## Why this matters for companies

Where an automated system is used in permits, registrations, tenders, tax matters, inspections or other proceedings affecting a company's interests, several questions become worth checking.

Does the decision carry an indication that AI was used? Did an authorised official review and confirm it? What information was it based on? Was there a means of correcting inaccurate information and reprocessing the result? Each of these may matter if an administrative dispute arises.

That said, a breach of one of these requirements does not, by itself, make the decision automatically void. The legal consequence has to be assessed under the applicable administrative and procedural rules in the circumstances of the case.

The rules are also directly relevant to technology companies supplying AI systems to the public sector. Audit logging, source traceability, human review, reprocessing of erroneous data and marking of AI-generated output increasingly need to be addressed in both technical specifications and contracts.

## Conclusion

Mongolia does not yet have a general AI law covering the private sector. But the introduction of statutory requirements for human oversight, transparency, traceability and non-discrimination in the state's use of AI is a significant development in technology law.
