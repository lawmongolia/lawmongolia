When a tax audit results in additional tax, penalties and late-payment charges, the taxpayer may dispute the assessment if it considers the decision unlawful or unjustified.

However, challenging a tax assessment is subject to procedural deadlines and, in specified circumstances, an advance-payment requirement. The filing of a complaint does not automatically suspend the underlying tax-payment obligation.

## The deadline for challenging a tax reassessment

Article 43.1 of Mongolia's General Tax Law allows a taxpayer to challenge all or part of a tax reassessment act by submitting a complaint to the competent Tax Dispute Resolution Council within **30 days of receiving the act**.

The date of receipt must be distinguished from the commencement of the audit or the date on which preliminary findings were discussed.

Article 43.2 also makes clear that failure to receive an act within the prescribed period does not necessarily prevent the applicable complaint deadline from being calculated under the law.

Businesses should therefore maintain accurate records of official notices, service of documents and electronic correspondence.

## The 10% advance-payment requirement

Under Article 47.3, a taxpayer challenging a tax reassessment act must make an advance payment equal to **10% of the disputed amount of reassessed tax**, subject to a maximum advance payment of **MNT 100 million**.

The calculation requires particular care.

The provision refers to the disputed amount of reassessed tax, rather than automatically applying 10% to the combined amount of tax, penalties and late-payment charges.

Where a taxpayer disputes only part of the reassessed tax, that disputed amount is relevant to calculating the statutory advance payment.

Making the advance payment does not mean that the taxpayer has accepted the lawfulness of the assessment. It is a statutory requirement connected with the dispute procedure.

## Can the advance payment be recovered?

Article 47.4 provides a mechanism for addressing the refund of advance payments where the Tax Dispute Resolution Council or a court finally upholds the taxpayer's complaint or claim, in accordance with the refund provisions of Article 49.

Recovery is therefore linked to the outcome of the proceedings and the applicable tax-accounting and refund procedures. It should not be treated as automatic or unconditional.

## Administrative review and court proceedings

Articles 46 and 47 establish the Tax Dispute Resolution Council framework.

The competent council depends on the taxpayer's administrative jurisdiction, including whether the taxpayer falls under the authority responsible for large taxpayers.

The council may amend, cancel, uphold or, in prescribed circumstances, suspend and return the contested tax act.

Under Articles 47.15 and 47.16, a complaint is generally to be decided within **30 days**, subject to one extension of up to a further 30 days.

If the taxpayer disagrees with the council's decision, Article 47.19 provides a right to apply to the court within **30 days after receiving that decision**.

## An appeal does not automatically suspend payment

Article 12.1.9 of the General Tax Law provides that filing a complaint against an unlawful decision or action of the tax authority does not, by itself, suspend payment of assessed tax.

Accordingly, litigation strategy and cash-flow planning must be considered separately.

The taxpayer should assess not only the substantive grounds of its challenge but also the financial consequences of the assessment and any relevant statutory collection or protective procedures.

## Evidence is often decisive

Tax disputes frequently involve disagreements about the legal and economic character of transactions, rather than the tax rate alone.

Contracts, accounting records, invoices, payment instructions, banking records and evidence of actual performance may all be relevant to whether the authority has correctly characterised a transaction.

Article 41.12 recognises the taxpayer's right to present explanations and evidence during the audit and to protect its interests through an authorised representative or professional adviser.

A defensible tax position therefore requires an integrated assessment of the applicable law, transaction records, the authority's calculations and procedural compliance.

## Conclusion

The right to challenge a tax reassessment is an important legal safeguard, but its exercise requires careful attention to the statutory process.

The **30-day complaint period, 10% advance-payment requirement, competent administrative body and subsequent court deadline** may materially affect the conduct of the dispute.

Businesses should therefore establish their legal position and preserve supporting evidence promptly rather than waiting until the final stages of administrative or judicial proceedings.
