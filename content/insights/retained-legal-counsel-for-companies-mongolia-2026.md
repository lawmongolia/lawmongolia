A company does not need legal advice only when litigation begins. Supply contracts, employment decisions, collections, licences, customer data, finance and negotiations all create legal consequences. Some businesses maintain an in-house legal team and instruct external specialists for specific transactions. Others contract a firm for continuing support. A quoted monthly fee, however, says little about the quality or scope of the service unless the engagement is clearly defined.

Mongolian law distinguishes ordinary legal advisory work from the regulated activities of an **advocate entitled to represent a client in court**. Articles 3.1 and 3.2 of the [Law on Advocacy](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14859) separate the statute's special regime for advocates from other professional legal services such as opinions and document drafting. A company engaging external legal counsel should therefore identify the contractual provider, the individuals responsible for the work and what happens if a matter becomes contentious.

## Define deliverables rather than promising “all legal work”

An effective retainer identifies the kinds of issues the business actually faces. A technology platform may need product terms, personal-data advice and commercial agreements. A trading company may need procurement, distribution, leases and receivables work. Neither benefits from an engagement letter that merely promises advice on “all legal matters”.

At a minimum, consider defining contract drafting and review, oral and written opinions, internal policies, correspondence with regulators, support during negotiations, legislative monitoring and the initial assessment of disputes. State whether the agreed deliverable is a marked-up agreement, a memorandum, a short recommendation, a meeting or representation before a public authority. Nominate who in the client organisation may instruct counsel and approve a final position.

## A fixed retainer does not necessarily mean unlimited work

A monthly fixed fee may be commercially efficient, but the engagement should explain how work volume and urgency are managed. Reviewing five standard supplier agreements is not equivalent to negotiating a cross-border investment transaction consisting of several lengthy documents. Specify any monthly limits, exclusions, response standards for normal and urgent requests and the process for approving additional fees.

Clear boundaries benefit both sides. They make the service predictable and reduce pressure to produce rushed or superficial advice. Consider also whether a monthly status report is appropriate, particularly where the business has multiple subsidiaries or substantial regulatory obligations.

## Distinguish an advisory company from an advocacy entity

Article 18 of the Law on Advocacy permits advocates to practise individually, in contractual association or through a legal entity. Article 19 imposes specific requirements on an **advocacy legal entity**, including an approved legal form and use of the Mongolian word for advocacy in its registered name. An ordinary consulting company does not automatically acquire the status of an advocacy entity simply because it employs or engages lawyers.

If a dispute requires representation by a licensed advocate, verify the advocate's authorisation and document the relevant engagement and procedural authority. A payment to a consulting company alone should not be treated as proof that an identified individual has authority to appear in court. The contractual and professional confidentiality frameworks should also be examined rather than presumed identical.

## Confidentiality, conflicts and information security

External counsel often handles pricing, staff records, allegations of wrongdoing, settlement strategy and confidential negotiations. A workable agreement should address authorised access, permitted use, secure transmission, use of subcontractors, retention, return or deletion of materials and disclosures required by law. A generic sentence promising confidentiality may not allocate responsibility adequately where many staff members or systems process the same information.

A firm should also assess conflicts of interest before accepting a new instruction and when new counterparties or opposing interests emerge. In advocacy engagements, the specific conflict and secrecy duties under the Law on Advocacy must be considered. For an advisory arrangement, the contract should still specify how conflicts will be identified, notified and resolved.

## Is litigation included in the monthly fee?

Only if the parties say so. Early claim assessment and preparation of a response may fit within routine advisory work, while hearings, interim applications, appeals and enforcement may require distinct budgets and specialist representation. The engagement should separate these stages and establish whether the client may appoint separate litigation counsel.

Where professional advocacy is instructed, Article 22 of the [Law on Advocacy](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14859) requires an engagement in writing or electronic form. The statutory content includes the services, fees, reimbursable expenses, responsibilities and termination arrangements. These safeguards should not be overlooked simply because the same client already has a general advisory agreement.

## Measure results, not just billable activity

A retained legal provider adds value when risks are identified before signing, response deadlines are met, management receives usable advice and legislative changes turn into practical compliance steps. A concise monthly report can distinguish completed work, outstanding approvals, regulatory deadlines and high-priority decisions.

Before selecting a law firm in Mongolia, management should answer three questions: what problems require regular advice; when must the matter be referred to a licensed advocate; and who within the company owns implementation? These questions provide a sounder basis for evaluating a retainer than price alone.
