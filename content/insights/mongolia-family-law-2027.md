On 3 July 2026 the State Great Khural adopted a revised version of the Family Law. Its main provisions apply from 1 January 2027. The new law reworks marriage, divorce, custody, maintenance, family property, marriage contracts and the property relations of couples who have not registered a marriage.

## Pre-marriage health testing clarified

Those intending to marry are required, before registering the marriage, to undergo health testing and obtain a certificate, so that each is informed about sexually transmitted infections and HIV/AIDS.

The purpose is not to prohibit marriage but to ensure that people who are about to marry have certain health information about one another.

## DNA testing is not compulsory

One provision that drew public attention is pre-marriage DNA testing.

Article 5.4 provides that those intending to marry "may undergo" genetic DNA testing in order to establish whether they are related. It is an option, not an obligation. It also takes effect on 1 January 2028 rather than in 2027 with the rest of the law.

The idea that "everyone marrying from 2027 must take a DNA test" does not follow from the law.

## Marriage contracts regulated in detail

One of the larger changes is the marriage contract. Those about to marry may conclude a contract governing their property rights and obligations before registering the marriage, and spouses may do so at any time afterwards.

A marriage contract must be made in writing and certified by a notary. Where it contains terms concerning rights in immovable property, a preliminary entry must also be made in the state register of rights.

A marriage contract may deal with how joint family property is allocated, which property is separate, how property is divided once the marriage ends, and how family expenses are borne.

It cannot release a parent from the duty to maintain a child, restrict parental rights, put one spouse in a plainly worse position, or restrict personal rights by contract.

## Property disputes between cohabiting couples specifically addressed

The property of people who have lived together for a long period without registering a marriage is a frequent source of dispute in practice. The new law addresses it specifically.

Where all of the following conditions are met, a court may resolve a dispute over property rights arising during the period of cohabitation under the provisions governing joint family property:

- neither party has a registered marriage to anyone else;
- they have lived together continuously for at least five years;
- the property was jointly owned or was acquired during the cohabitation;
- they were registered at the same address of residence.

This is not to be read as "five years of living together counts automatically as marriage". The law sets out the procedure for resolving property disputes between cohabiting couples who meet those conditions.

The law also states directly that cohabitation alone does not give rise to inheritance rights between the parties, and that there is no duty to maintain one another once the cohabitation ends.

## Divorce procedure clarified

Where the spouses agree to divorce, have no children under 18 and no property dispute, they may divorce administratively through the state registration authority. Where those conditions are not met, the matter is decided by a court.

The effect is to keep an agreed divorce with no children and no property dispute out of court proceedings.

## A parent living apart may agree contact with the child by contract

A parent who has divorced, previously cohabited, or otherwise lives apart from their child may agree by contract the terms, timing and duration of contact and visits, and the rights, obligations and responsibilities relating to the child's upbringing.

The agreement must be made in writing and certified by a notary, and its terms must not worsen the rights and lawful interests of the child.

This allows contact with a child after divorce to be set out in a document rather than left to an oral understanding.

## Existing maintenance orders may be brought into line with the new law

Where maintenance was ordered by a court before the new law took effect, the recipient or their legal representative may bring a claim within twelve months from 1 January 2027 to have the amount of future maintenance re-determined under the new law.

The methodology for determining child maintenance under the new law is to be approved by 1 January 2027.

Anyone with an existing maintenance order should therefore check separately how the new rules apply to them during 2027.

## Conclusion

The revised Family Law taking effect in 2027 is not only a reform of marriage and divorce. It regulates in more detail a set of matters that regularly give rise to disputes in practice: marriage contracts, the property relations of cohabiting couples, contact between a child and a parent living apart, and maintenance.

On DNA testing, it is worth not misreading the law. The test is optional, and the relevant provision applies from 2028.
