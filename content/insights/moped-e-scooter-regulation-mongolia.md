On 12 June 2026 amendments to the Law on Road Traffic Safety introduced specific rules for the use of mopeds and electric scooters. They settle at the level of primary legislation a number of questions that had been unclear in practice: the age at which they may be ridden, training, vehicle registration, shared-scooter services and liability.

## Which vehicles the rules cover

The law defines mopeds and the scooters treated as mopeds by reference to technical specifications. A vehicle with two or more wheels falls within the category where it has an internal combustion engine of no more than 50 cc, or an electric motor rated between 0.25 kW and 4 kW, and a maximum design speed of no more than 50 km/h.

Not every two-wheeled electric vehicle is therefore a moped. Motor rating and maximum design speed have to be checked before the category can be established.

## Mopeds and scooters may be ridden from age 18

One of the most important changes is the age limit. Mopeds and scooters within the category above may now be ridden in traffic in Mongolia by a person aged 18 or over.

Parents and guardians are also placed under a duty not to allow a child under 18 to ride a moped or scooter in traffic.

A corresponding penalty has been added to the Law on Infringements. Where a child under 18 rides a moped or scooter in traffic, the parent or guardian is fined an amount equal to 200 units.

## Reaching 18 is not enough — training is required

Being 18 does not mean a person may ride a moped without preparation. A rider must have completed training under the rules and curriculum for driver training and be recorded in the database of the driver training organisation.

However, a resolution of the State Great Khural on implementation of the law directed that a citizen holding a driving licence under the Road Traffic Rules be treated as having completed that training.

A person with a valid car driving licence and a person without one are therefore not in the same position as regards the requirement to ride a moped.

## Mopeds and scooters must be registered

The owner of a moped, or a scooter treated as one, is now under a duty to register the vehicle in accordance with the applicable registration rules. This requirement has applied since 30 June 2026.

The implementation resolution also directed the relevant bodies to bring mopeds and scooters already in use into the registration system and to revise the registration procedure.

The effect is to move mopeds and electric scooters out of a position where they circulated in traffic unregistered and with no identified owner, and into a system of registration and oversight.

## Riding across a pedestrian crossing is prohibited

Riding a moped or scooter across a pedestrian crossing is an infringement. The rider is fined an amount equal to 50 units.

A person pushing a moped or scooter falls within the definition of a pedestrian. Dismounting and pushing the vehicle across the crossing therefore complies with the law.

## Specific requirements for shared-scooter operators

The activity of companies that rent mopeds and scooters to the public for a fee is also specifically regulated. Such an operator must conclude an agreement with the governor of the aimag or the capital city before operating. That agreement may set the service charge, fees, the zone of operation and the location of parking areas.

The operator must register its vehicles, park them in approved locations, process customer personal data in accordance with the law, and hand over batteries and hazardous waste to designated facilities. It must also restrict, by technological means, the use of vehicles by anyone under 18 or who has not completed the required training. Liability insurance is required to cover loss caused to others through a failure to meet these obligations.

An operator that has not registered its vehicles, or has not put technological measures in place to prevent use by a person failing the age or training requirements, is liable to a fine equal to 500 units. A fine of 50 units has been added to the Law on Infringements for parking vehicles in an unauthorised location.

## Conclusion

The new rules on mopeds and electric scooters did more than change the minimum age. Rider training, vehicle registration, parental responsibility, the use of pedestrian crossings and the obligations of rental operators have all been settled together.

For individuals, what matters is not only whether they are 18 but whether they meet the training requirement and whether the vehicle is registered. For shared-scooter operators, the local agreement, registration, parking, the technology for checking a user's age and training, and insurance are all now part of operating lawfully.
